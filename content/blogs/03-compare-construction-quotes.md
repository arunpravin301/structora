# How to Compare Construction Quotes in Tamil Nadu: 10 Questions Every Homeowner Should Ask

Imagine you have two quotations on your dining table for your new 1,200 sq ft house. Builder A quotes **₹2,510 per sq ft**. Builder B quotes **₹2,300 per sq ft**.

On a calculator, Builder B looks cheaper by ₹210 per sq ft—an apparent saving of **₹2.52 Lakhs**.

Yet time after time across Tamil Nadu, we meet homeowners who chose the lowest square-foot rate on paper, only to spend ₹4 Lakhs *more* by the time they received their house keys. Why? Because a square-foot rate is meaningless until you know **how the area is measured, which steel and cement brands go into the structure, what base rates are allowed for tiles and doors, and which civil works are left out**.

Before you sign a construction agreement, give every builder the exact same floor plan and ask them to answer these ten questions in writing.

## 1. How exactly have you calculated the chargeable built-up area?

A lower per-sq-ft rate can easily produce a higher total bill if the contractor inflates the chargeable area.

Suppose Builder A prices your home at ₹2,510 × **1,200 sq ft** = **₹30.12 Lakhs** based strictly on outer-to-outer roof slab measurement. Suppose Builder B quotes ₹2,350/sq ft, but adds extra percentage loadings for sunshades, weather projections, double-height cutouts, and open sit-outs, pushing the chargeable area to **1,320 sq ft** (**₹31.02 Lakhs**).

Ask every builder for a floor-wise area breakup showing:
- Ground-floor and first-floor roof slab area
- Staircase headroom (`mumty`) area on the terrace
- Portico (covered car parking) and balcony measurement rules

## 2. Which primary steel and cement grades are specified for structural work?

Once your columns and roof slabs are plastered over, you can never swap out weak steel or inferior cement. That is why low-ball quotes almost always cut corners in the structural skeleton first.

Check that your quotation explicitly names:
- **TMT Steel Bars:** Primary ISI-certified **Fe550D grade TMT steel** from tier-1 manufacturers like **TATA Tiscon or JSW Neosteel**—not generic secondary ("rolling mill" / bazaar) steel rods.
- **Cement Grade & Brand:** **53-Grade OPC/PPC cement** from **UltraTech, Dalmia, or Ramco** for all structural concrete (footings, columns, beams, and slabs), and 43-Grade for masonry and plastering.
- **Concrete Mix & Aggregates:** Proper M20 grade design mix ratios, washed M-sand for concrete, and double-washed P-sand for smooth internal plastering.

## 3. What is the exact rupee allowance for tiles, sanitaryware, and joinery?

Words like *"First-quality vitrified tiles"* or *"Branded sanitaryware"* in a quotation leave huge room for disputes later. Always insist on **written rupee base rates (allowances)** per sq ft or per bathroom.

Compare two quotes side by side on these five high-ticket items:
1. **Main floor tiles:** Does the quote allow ₹55/sq ft or ₹80–₹100/sq ft? Does that rate include tile spacers, adhesive, and skirting?
2. **Main door frame & shutter:** Is the main entrance door genuine **first-class Burma/Indian Teak** with brass/SS hardware, or a treated country-wood frame with a veneer flush door?
3. **Windows:** Are UPVC windows (with 5mm toughened glass and MS safety grills) included, or basic aluminium sliding frames?
4. **Bathroom fittings:** What is the exact budget allocated per bathroom for wall-mounted closets, diverters, and showers (Parryware / Hindware / Jaquar / Kohler)?
5. **Electrical wires & switches:** Are flame-retardant (FRLS) cables from **Finolex / Orbit** and modular switches from **Legrand / Schneider / GM** specified by name?

## 4. What sits outside the square-foot rate (Exclusions)?

Even when a builder advertises a *"100% Turnkey Package"*, certain plot-dependent items sit outside the base square-foot rate. A transparent builder will hand you a clear exclusion schedule on Day 1 rather than surprising you midway through construction.

Ask each builder to mark whether these items are **Included** or **Charged Separately**:
- Compound wall (per running foot) and MS/SS main gate
- Underground RCC water sump (per litre) and septic tank
- Borewell drilling, submersible motor, and temporary EB service connection
- Local body / DTCP building plan approval fees
- Extra basement height above 3 feet from road level
- Modular kitchen cabinets, bedroom wardrobes, and false ceiling

## 5. What foundation depth and soil conditions does the quote assume?

A standard package quote assumes normal firm soil at a standard excavation depth (usually 4 to 5 feet) and a standard basement height (usually 3 feet above road level).

Ask your builder:
- *"Have you inspected my plot's soil strata and road level?"*
- *"If our structural design requires deeper footings, pile foundations (common in clayey delta belts or black cotton soil), or an extra foot of basement filling, what is the unit rate for that additional work?"*

Agreeing on unit rates before excavation prevents arbitrary extra bills when foundation pits are open.

## 6. Is the payment schedule tied to actual site milestones?

Never sign a construction contract where payments are demanded on fixed calendar dates regardless of site progress, or where 30–40% of the contract value is collected before the basement is even completed.

A fair, bank-friendly payment schedule is strictly **milestone-linked**:
1. Mobilization & structural design advance
2. Foundation & footing concrete completion
3. Plinth beam & basement filling completion
4. Ground-floor lintel level
5. Ground-floor roof slab casting
6. Brick masonry & internal/external plastering
7. Flooring, electrical & plumbing rough-in
8. Painting, joinery & final key handover

## 7. How are mid-project design changes or material upgrades billed?

Almost every family makes a few upgrades during a 9-month build—perhaps adding a puja niche, choosing a larger 4×2 ft tile in the living hall, or adding extra electrical points for a home office.

Ask how variations are handled:
- Will you receive a **written cost difference note** (new item cost minus the package base allowance) *before* the material is ordered?
- Who on the builder's side and who in your family signs off on changes so verbal instructions on site never turn into disputed bills at handover?

## 8. Will a qualified civil engineer supervise my site daily?

There is a massive difference between an **engineering construction company** and a labour-contract middleman who visits your site once a week on a motorcycle.

Ask directly:
- *"Who is the named site engineer assigned to my house?"*
- *"How many quality checks do you run before casting a roof slab or covering concealed plumbing lines?"* (At Structora, our engineers execute a **200+ point quality checklist** on every project.)
- *"Can I visit two of your currently active sites and talk to the homeowners directly?"*

## 9. What is the realistic completion timeline, and what does "handover" include?

A standard independent house (`G` or `G+1`) in Tamil Nadu takes **8 to 10 months** to build properly when concrete curing cycles, plaster drying times, and finishing stages are respected without rushing.

Clarify what constitutes final completion:
- Does handover include acid-wash/deep cleaning of floors, pressure-testing all plumbing lines, testing every electrical circuit, and handing over as-built structural and plumbing drawings?

## 10. What structural warranty and post-handover support do you provide?

A relationship with your builder shouldn't end the day you perform your *Grihapravesam* (housewarming).

Ask for the written warranty terms covering:
- **Structural integrity warranty** against foundation settlement or RCC structural defects.
- **Waterproofing warranty** on the terrace weathering course, sunken bathrooms, and overhead water tank.
- **Defect liability response:** Who do you call if a door latch needs adjustment or a plumbing joint drips three months after moving in?

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why do house construction rates in Tamil Nadu vary from ₹2,100 to ₹3,200+ per sq ft?
The per-sq-ft rate depends on four factors: (1) primary ISI steel and 53-grade cement vs unbranded materials, (2) the rupee allowances for tiles, teakwood doors, UPVC windows, and sanitaryware, (3) how the builder calculates chargeable slab and projection areas, and (4) whether qualified civil engineers supervise the site daily.

### Is the lowest per-sq-ft construction quote actually the cheapest?
Rarely. Quotes with unusually low square-foot rates typically recover their margin by inflating the chargeable area measurement, setting unrealistic base rates (e.g., ₹45/sq ft for tiles), or charging heavily for undocumented extras mid-build.

### Should I ask for specific material brand names in my construction agreement?
Yes. Always ensure your agreement specifies exact brands, grades, and rupee allowances—such as TATA/JSW Fe550D steel, UltraTech/Dalmia 53-grade cement, Finolex cables, and the exact per-sq-ft tile rate—rather than vague phrases like "ISI brand or equivalent."

### How many construction quotes should I compare before choosing a builder?
Comparing **two to three detailed, itemised quotations** based on the exact same floor plan and soil assumptions is ideal. Always pair your quote comparison with an in-person visit to each builder's ongoing and completed residential sites.
