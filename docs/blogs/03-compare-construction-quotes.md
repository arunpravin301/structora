# How to Compare Construction Quotes: 10 Questions to Ask Your Builder

Two builders have quoted for your house. One is cheaper by ₹150 per sq ft.

For 1,200 sq ft, that is ₹1.8 lakh. It is enough to deserve a careful look. But it only represents a saving if both prices cover the same area, materials and work.

Before choosing, send both builders the same drawings and ask them to answer these ten questions in writing. You will learn more from those answers than from comparing package names.

## 1. What area have you used to calculate the price?

Ask for the measured area on each floor and the rule used for balconies, staircases, parking and terrace structures.

A lower rate can produce a higher bill if it is applied to a larger chargeable area. For example, a hypothetical quote of ₹2,400 × 1,300 sq ft totals ₹31.2 lakh. Another at ₹2,500 × 1,200 sq ft totals ₹30 lakh.

That is not evidence that either builder is wrong. It tells you that their measurement assumptions need explaining.

Use the same agreed area statement for the comparison.

## 2. Which drawings and specifications does this price cover?

The quote should identify the drawings used for pricing and their revision dates. If one builder has priced an earlier layout, the two totals may describe different houses.

Ask for a written specification alongside the price. It should give enough detail to understand the proposed structure and finishes, with technical requirements established by the responsible designer.

“Premium materials” is too vague to price against another offer. You need to know the actual items and allowances.

## 3. What does each material allowance buy?

Choose a few items you care about and follow them through the quotation: floor tiles, bathroom fittings, kitchen countertop, doors, windows and electrical fittings.

For each one, ask whether the allowance is per square foot, per item, per bathroom or a total project amount. Check whether it includes supply, installation, transport, wastage and taxes.

If the specification says “or equivalent,” ask who approves a substitute and how equivalence is judged.

You should be able to choose a product from a shop and understand how it affects the contract amount before it is purchased.

## 4. What is excluded, and what will those items cost?

Request a separate exclusion list even if the package is described as turnkey.

Check the compound wall, gates, water storage, drainage arrangements, external paving, approvals, power and water connections, interiors and appliances. Mark each item as included, separately priced, owner-supplied or not required.

Your comparison should end with a total for the scope you want, rather than a package total with unresolved extras underneath it.

## 5. What site conditions have you assumed?

Ask what the quote assumes about foundation work, ground levels, access and any existing structures.

Then ask what happens if the site investigation or design requires a change. How will extra quantities be measured? Who approves the rate? Will the revised price be issued before that work proceeds?

A fixed price can be useful, but only when you understand the work and assumptions behind it. A quotation made before key information is available should identify what remains provisional.

## 6. When are payments due, and what confirms each stage?

Ask for the amount or percentage payable at every stage, including the advance and final payment.

Each milestone should be understandable. What does “structure complete” include? Who checks the work before a stage payment is requested? How much notice will you receive?

If you are using a construction loan, compare the builder’s schedule with your lender’s release process. A mismatch can create a cash-flow problem even when the overall budget is sufficient.

Keep receipts and an updated record of payments against the agreed stages.

## 7. How are changes priced?

Changes are easier to manage when the process is agreed before anyone wants one.

Suppose you decide to add a window, move a wall or upgrade the flooring. Ask the builder to state the cost difference, any credit for omitted work, and the effect on time before you approve the change.

Decide who can authorise variations. A message from a visiting relative should not become an unexpected bill.

The record can be straightforward: a clear description, revised price, time impact and written approval from the agreed decision-maker.

## 8. Who supervises the work and checks quality?

Ask who your site contact will be and how often the responsible supervisor or engineer attends. Clarify which inspections and records are included in the service.

Request an explanation of how material deliveries, concealed work and relevant quality checks are recorded. Before work is covered up, how will the necessary checks be completed?

If you visit a project, look beyond the finished elevation. Ask how the team communicates decisions and keeps track of changes. Those conversations help you assess the working relationship you would be buying.

## 9. What does the completion date depend on?

Ask for a programme showing the main stages and the information or selections needed from you.

The agreement should explain how delays are reported and how changes in scope, approvals, owner decisions or other events affect the programme. Avoid relying on a verbal promise tied only to a festival or family event.

Handover also needs a definition. Does it include cleaning, testing, correcting listed defects and handing over documents? If those activities sit after the quoted completion milestone, your moving date may need to account for them.

## 10. What happens after handover?

Ask for the defect-reporting process, response arrangements and the written terms of any warranty.

Clarify which items are covered by the builder and which depend on a manufacturer’s warranty. Get the duration, exclusions and contact details in writing.

Before the final settlement, agree how remaining work will be recorded and closed. Ask which approved drawings, product documents, test records and operating information will be handed over where applicable.

A reassuring promise becomes more useful when you know exactly how to use it.

## Put the answers on one page

Make a comparison sheet with a column for each builder. Use rows for the chargeable area, base price, inclusions, exclusions, provisional items, taxes, payment stages, programme, supervision and defect support.

Leave an unanswered item marked “not confirmed.” Putting zero beside it makes the total look more certain than it is.

Once both builders have answered, compare the price for the same scope. A higher total may include work the lower quote leaves out; a lower total may genuinely offer better value. The written details will help you decide which is which.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the lowest square-foot rate the cheapest option?

Only when the area, scope, specifications and additional charges are comparable. Calculate the complete price for the work you need.

### Should I ask for brand names?

Ask for brands where relevant, along with grades, models, dimensions, allowances and installation details. A brand name alone may still leave several products to choose from.

### Can a fixed-price contract still have extra charges?

The agreement may allow changes for specified circumstances or owner-requested variations. Read those clauses and the exclusions before deciding how fixed the total really is.

### How many quotations should I get?

Get enough detailed quotations to make a meaningful comparison. Two or three complete responses based on the same drawings are more useful than a long list of informal rates.
