# Planning to Build a House in Tamil Nadu? A Checklist Before Construction Starts

You may already know how many bedrooms you want and have a folder full of house photographs. Before those ideas become a construction contract, a few less exciting details need attention.

Does the measured plot match the documents? Can the design you want be approved there? Who will prepare the structural drawings? Where will construction water come from?

This checklist helps you prepare for those discussions. It is a planning guide; the documents and permissions for your particular property should be confirmed by the relevant local authority and the professionals handling your project.

## 1. Put the plot records together

Create one folder for the registered ownership documents, relevant earlier documents, encumbrance information, available revenue records, survey information and any existing layout or building approvals.

Treat this as a preparation list, not a universal application checklist. The records needed vary with the property and the work proposed.

Tamil Nadu provides access to land-record extracts such as Patta, Chitta, FMB and TSLR through its e-services system. The [government’s land-record service page](https://coimbatore.nic.in/service/eservices-anytime-anywhere-land/) links to that facility. Ask a qualified property lawyer to review the ownership position and any discrepancies; downloading a record is only one part of that work.

If the property has an existing building, find its approved plans and available construction records too. They may matter when planning an alteration or extension.

## 2. Have the site measured

A sketch copied from an old document is not enough to settle the practical layout.

Arrange for the boundaries, dimensions, access and relevant site levels to be checked. Compare the measured information with the records before drawing a house that relies on every last foot of frontage.

Then visit the plot with the person preparing the design. Discuss delivery access, nearby structures, overhead lines, drainage and the available space for storing materials.

Bring photographs if you cannot visit together, but leave room for a proper site inspection. A view from the road will not answer every question.

## 3. Write a brief about everyday life

Start with the things the house must do.

Do you need a bedroom on the ground floor? Will a parent have difficulty using stairs? Is the kitchen used by more than one person? Do you need parking for a car, two-wheelers or both? Where will clothes dry?

List future requirements separately. A home office, rental portion or additional floor can affect decisions made much earlier than expected.

A useful brief might read: “Two bedrooms now, one on the ground floor; parking for one car; a utility space beside the kitchen; assess provision for a future first floor.”

That gives the designer something concrete to work with. Photographs can then help explain your preferences for light, materials and appearance.

## 4. Confirm the approval route for the actual plot

Ask the professional handling your application to identify the competent authority, applicable process, required documents and responsibilities.

Tamil Nadu has introduced a self-certification route for eligible residential proposals, described in the state’s [2025–26 housing policy note](https://tnhb.tn.gov.in/assets/documents/Policy%20Note%20%26%20Announcements/English%20Policy%20Note%202025-2026.pdf). That does not establish eligibility for your plot. Current conditions and the route applicable to your proposal need checking before you rely on it.

Avoid fixing an approval fee or completion date from somebody else’s project. Ask for your own application requirements and charges in writing.

Also agree who will keep copies of submissions, receipts, approved drawings and any subsequent permissions or inspection records required for the work.

## 5. Resolve the structure and ground conditions

Tell the structural engineer what you plan to build now and what you may want later.

Ask what ground investigation is appropriate, what information is needed for the design, and which drawings must be ready before construction begins. A neighbour’s foundation is not a design for your house.

Check the scope of your selected construction package. If soil investigation or structural drawings are not included in the price, establish who will arrange and pay for them. A missing commercial allowance does not settle whether a technical service is needed.

The [BIS homeowner resource collection](https://www.bis.gov.in/guide-for-homeowners-and-homebuyers/?lang=en) includes separate guides on permits and constructing an independent house. Use them to prepare questions for your professionals, rather than as drawings or instructions for carrying out structural work yourself.

## 6. Coordinate the drawings before work starts

A floor plan shows rooms. Construction also needs decisions about the structure, openings, electrical points, plumbing and other services.

Ask who checks that these decisions fit together. Where does the water tank go? How will pipes be routed? Does the staircase work with the floor heights? Are there provisions for air-conditioning, an inverter or other equipment you expect to install?

Keep a dated set of the drawings approved for construction. If something changes, make sure the site team receives the revised version and knows which earlier drawing it replaces.

This is especially useful when several family members are involved in decisions.

## 7. Separate the house price from the full project budget

Request a written list of inclusions and exclusions. Add the excluded work you actually need to your budget before deciding whether the project is affordable.

For example, Structora’s Kumbakonam schedule lists items such as the compound wall, sump, plan approval and modular kitchen outside the square-foot package. A project in another location needs its own schedule checked.

Ask for the expected payment stages too. Your savings or loan releases need to be available when those stages are reached.

## 8. Agree how the job will be managed

Before paying an advance, settle who supervises the work, who answers your questions, and how decisions are recorded.

The agreement should address the scope, payment stages, expected programme, changes, delays, defect correction and handover. Have unclear or consequential contract terms reviewed before signing.

Choose one family contact to approve changes. A casual request to move a wall or add a bathroom can affect several trades and the price. Ask for the cost and time implications before approving it.

## Take a useful folder to the first meeting

Bring the plot location, available records, measured dimensions if you have them, photographs, a short room brief and a realistic total budget. Include your preferred timing and any future-floor plans.

You do not need to have every answer. The point is to make the unanswered questions visible early.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I finalise the layout before checking the site?

You can explore ideas, but treat them as preliminary until the plot information, site constraints and applicable requirements have been checked.

### Should future floors be discussed now?

Yes. Tell the design team about the intended future use during the initial brief so it can assess the structural and planning implications.

### What if the package does not include a soil test or structural drawings?

Ask the engineer what the project requires and arrange the necessary services explicitly. Confirm the responsibility and cost in the quotation.

### Is a standard online checklist enough for approval?

No single checklist covers every property and proposal. Confirm the current requirements with the relevant authority and the professional handling your application.
